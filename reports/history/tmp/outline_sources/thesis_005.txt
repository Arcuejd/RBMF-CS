Robust Entropy Weight-Driven Sparse Deployment 
Optimization for Oceanic Sensing Nodes with Polar 
Applications 
ABSTRACT 
    Ocean sensing plays a strateg ically important role in revealing oceanic physical laws, 
developing marine resources, ensuring ship navigation safety and maritime traffic efficiency , 
and supporting marine industries and agriculture. The deployment strategy of sensing nodes 
directly affects the monitoring efficiency and deployment cost of ocean monitoring systems , 
consequently affecting the perception efficiency of maritime traffic environments . With the 
advancement of data science technologies and the improvement of global ocean observation 
systems, optimizing node deployment based on data analysis has become possible. This 
thesis focuses on solving the problem of determining the optimal set of node deployment 
locations, aiming to achieve efficient measurement and high-precision reconstruction of ocean 
states through sparse measurement. 
To address the strong spatiotemporal correlation of marine environmental data (e.g., 
temperature, salinity), this thesis employs spatial feature selection to identify critical sensitivity 
zones, enabling sparse deployment of sensing nodes  and thereby facilitating efficient ocean 
environment monitoring with a minimal number of nodes . The goal of sparse deployment of 
ocean sensing nodes is to select the optimal node locations and quantities and to establish the 
optimal mapping relationship between low-dimensional sampling and full-state data, achieving 
accurate full -state estimation based on limited measurements. However, current sparse 
deployment of sensing nodes faces the following bottlenecks: (1) Sparse deployment relies on 
full-state ocean data generated by model interpolation, making it sensitive to data noise when 
reconstructing data with low-dimensional sampling of deployed  nodes; (2) Deployment 
strategies are usually based on single -physical-quantity ocean datasets, lacking collaborative 
optimization for multi -physical-quantity joint reconstruction; (3) Data feature selection 
mechanisms overlook the trade-off between the amount of information acquired by the system 
and the associated costs.
