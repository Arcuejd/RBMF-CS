2. Deployment Optimization under Non -Gaussian Noise: To address the sensitivity of 
existing sparse node deployment to non -Gaussian noise when using  Frobenius norm to 
evaluate reconstruction error, a robust deployment optimization method based on the 
Maximum Correntropy Criterion (MCC) is proposed. Based on the aforementioned robust 
sparse deployment model, MCC is used to evaluate reconstruction error, and the local 
geometric features of the data are integrated to establish a robust sparse deployment 
optimization model based on MCC. Subsequently, a Robust Sensing Node Selection based on 
MCC (MCC_RSS) algorithm is proposed, which minimizes the impact of noise through 
iterative solving of the selection matrix and noise weight matrix. Experiments conducted on 
different ocean datasets show that th e algorithm further enhances the robustness of sparse 
node deployment strategies in the presence of outliers and non-Gaussian noise. 
3. Multi -Physical Quantities Node Sparse Deployment Optimization: For applications 
requiring multi -physical quantities data, a multi -physical quantities node sparse deployment 
optimization strategy based on a fractal autoencoder is proposed. A multi -physical quantities 
deployment model is established through the autoencoder architecture, using the average 
weight vector of location features to associate the latent variables of different physical 
quantities, achieving joint location feature selection. The proposed Multi -physical quantities 
Fractal Autoencoder (MFAE) has an innovative structure: each physical quantity's autoencoder 
includes a weight update layer and a feature selection layer based on average weights, while 
the decoder independently completes the nonlinear mapping from low -dimensional data to full 
state. The initialization method of the feature selection layer, combining average entropy 
weight distribution and noise distribution, effectively enhances the stability of the feature 
selection process. Experimental results show that MFAE significantly reduces the average 
reconstruction error of multi-physical data, achieving efficient joint feature extraction.
